# Feature: Core Play Loop

**Status:** shipped

## Summary
The **Core Play Loop** is the spine of a War match: the paced, single-player-vs-CPU sequence that carries a fresh deal through round after round of *flip → compare → award* until one side holds all 52 cards. It is the **basic gameplay implementation** — the layer that turns the deterministic engine's discrete legal transitions into a playable, animated experience without ever crossing the hard wall.

This feature ships in two phases, both inside this brief. **Phase A (offline MVP)** is the fully playable loop: a new presentation-tier subsystem, the **Round Director**, sits between the `WarMatch` funnel and the table view. The Director auto-advances the mechanical transitions the player should never tap — staging the next round (`start_round`) and awarding a resolved one (`resolve`) — on timed *beats*, and pauses for the player only at the signature decision points: the **flip moment** (reveal now, or first stake a Special Card) and the **WAR acknowledgment** (declare war to break a tie; deep runaway wars auto-advance). It owns no authoritative state — only a cosmetic *beat* derived from the engine phase plus a few animation timers — so a validator reconcile snaps cleanly by re-deriving the beat from the authoritative state.

**Phase B (online wire)** connects the loop to the authoritative validator. War already has the hard part — `WarMatch`'s optimistic-apply + reconcile core (with an `_apply_seq` stale-drop guard) and an injectable validator seam — so Phase B is *plumbing, not a rebuild*, modeled on the proven moveborne design: generate the godobuf transport bindings (today's hand-rolled JSON frame does not round-trip), thread `match_id`/`player_id` through every RPC, adopt a **server-assigned seed** at `InitMatch` (the deal — and thus the whole outcome — must not be client-shoppable), align the reconcile reply fields, and fire `CompleteMatch` on a terminal phase. Reconnect degrades to offline and re-inits.

Because the Director only chooses *which legal action to submit and when*, the action stream the validator re-runs stays byte-clean. War has no opponent decisions — both top cards are forced — so there is **no CPU AI**: the single deterministic engine plays both sides, and "vs CPU" is pacing and framing, not a second agent. This feature is the spine that sequences the two existing gameplay moments — Special Cards (the flip-moment hook) and the I-Declare-War Moment (the tie-breaker) — which plug into the beats the Director exposes.

## Components affected
- Round Director — presentation-tier play sequencer (new Node, game/ui/round_director.gd)
- Table view refit — render-only screen that forwards player intents to the Director
- WarMatch funnel wiring — drive via submit_action(WarActions.*), fix the broken table↔controller calls
- Beat machine + input-lock pacing — auto-advance staging/resolve, lock input while animating
- Round-result, WAR, and game-over beat presentation (replay / continue-to-lobby)

## Design constraints
1. Hard wall: the Round Director is pure presentation — it reads only state/events and submits only canonical WarActions through the single WarMatch funnel; it never re-implements rules or mutates hashed state.
2. Validator-clean action stream: the Director only chooses which LEGAL action to submit and when; auto-advanced actions (start_round, resolve) are deterministic and legal, so the validator re-runs an identical stream and the optimistic-then-reconcile model is unchanged.
3. Reconcile-safe pacing: the cosmetic beat is a pure function of engine phase; on every state_changed/reconciled the Director cancels pending timers and re-derives the beat from the authoritative state, never double-applying an action.
4. Single engine plays both sides: War gives the opponent no choices (both top cards are forced), so the basic loop has no CPU AI and the CPU plays no Special Cards; 'vs CPU' is pacing/framing only.
5. Offline-first and headless-drivable: the loop is fully playable with no validator attached (the deterministic engine is the source of truth), and the same actions remain drivable via WarDebug for the LLM/verifier path — the Director must never be REQUIRED for deterministic play.
6. Player decision points, with one refinement: the player acts at the flip moment (phase selected) and the WAR ack (phase war); every other transition auto-advances on a timed beat. Refinement for runaway wars — past war_depth WAR_AUTO_DEPTH (=3) the WAR ack also auto-advances after a short banner, so the WAR ack is a player decision point for shallow wars and an auto-beat for deep ones. (Later, once post-flip Special Cards exist, deep-war auto-ack defers while the player owns a relevant special.)
7. Transient beats own their own hold. ROUND_RESULT, WAR_BANNER and WAR_REVEALING are NOT engine phases, and WarMatch emits engine events BEFORE state_changed while an award/tie/decide returns the phase to dealt/war in the SAME apply. So a naive 'emit the transient in _on_event, then re-derive in _on_state' overwrites it instantly. The Director must gate the next auto-action behind the transient hold from ONE place: buffer the just-applied events and, on re-deriving the resting beat, hold the matching transient (result / banner / stake-reveal) for its duration before arming start_round/resolve/ack_war. A terminal award routes straight to GAME_OVER with no stranded ROUND_RESULT.
8. Online is wiring, not a rebuild (Phase B). Keep WarMatch's existing optimistic-apply + reconcile core and _apply_seq stale-drop guard UNTOUCHED; the work is transport + lifecycle — generate godobuf bindings, thread match_id/player_id on every RPC, adopt the server-assigned seed/state at InitMatch (init-first online), align the reconcile reply fields (read authoritative_state_json, trust the server ack, surface events_json without double-emitting optimistic events), and call CompleteMatch on a terminal phase. Modeled on moveborne's validated client; the Director stays transport-agnostic and the offline path is unchanged. Determinism parity is the load-bearing precondition (every honest move must hash identically on both sides) and the hashed state schema does NOT change.

## Resolved questions
1. **In the BASIC loop, should the flipped phase expose a post-flip Special window (rank_boost / steal_tie), or auto-resolve and defer that interaction to the Special Cards feature?** — _Defer. The basic loop auto-resolves the flipped phase after the reveal beat; the post-flip Special window (rank_boost / steal_tie) is owned by the Special Cards feature, which inserts a pause at the 'flipped' / 'war' beats the Director already exposes. The engine already permits post-flip specials in those phases, so this is a presentation seam, not an engine change — keeping 'basic' to one Special window (the flip moment)._
2. **Should staging auto-advance (dealt → selected with no tap) so the flip moment is the player's first interaction each round, rather than a separate 'deal' tap?** — _Yes. Entering phase 'dealt' (non-terminal) the Director auto-submits start_round after a short stage beat, so cards animate face-down onto the table and the loop settles at phase 'selected' — the flip moment is the player's first interaction each round. There is no separate 'deal' tap. (start_round is a deterministic, legal action, so the validator re-runs it identically.)_
3. **Does the CPU ever play Special Cards in the basic loop, or is the basic opponent purely the forced engine draw with no specials?** — _No. In the basic loop the CPU plays no Special Cards; the opponent is purely the forced engine draw. specials.cpu stays empty and the Director never submits a CPU special. CPU specials are a later balance/AI hook layered on top, not part of the basic spine._
4. **What are the exact beat durations (reveal, result hold, WAR banner, war stake reveal) for the right hypercasual game feel?** — _Ship the Balanced tempo profile as the default: stage 0.22s, card flip 0.30s, read-after-flip 0.35s, round-result hold 0.90s, WAR banner 1.10s, war stake reveal 0.45s (~1.8s of auto-beats per non-war round). A global SPEED multiplier and a per-war-depth decay layer on top. All are data-driven, presentation-only constants — they change WHEN an auto-action fires, never WHICH — so they are freely retunable after playtest without touching determinism parity. See the spec's Tempo and pacing section._
5. **For deep wars (high war_depth), is there a fast-forward / skip / auto-resolve control so pacing doesn't drag across repeated escalations?** — _Yes — three layers, all timing-only: (1) tap-to-skip fast-forwards any transient beat by firing its armed auto-action immediately; (2) war beats decay with depth, war_scale = max(0.30, 0.6^(depth-1)); (3) past war_depth 3 the DECLARE WAR prompt stops waiting and the Director auto-submits ack_war after a quick banner. Depths 1-3 still prompt, keeping the signature tap and drama for normal wars. None of this alters the action stream or hashes. See the spec's Tap-to-skip and deep-war auto-acceleration section._
6. **PLAY AGAIN reseed policy: a fresh local random seed each match offline is fine, but what is the seed source when online (client-proposed vs server-assigned)?** — _Online, the seed comes from the validator (server is the seed authority; clients cannot seed-shop for a favorable deal). A new online match adopts the validator-returned authoritative seed and deals from it, reproducing the byte-identical deal by the parity contract; offline, the client self-generates a fresh local seed. Implication: the validator's init/create-match call likely needs to RETURN a seed rather than only accept one — a Validator Client / Match State Store dependency, not an engine change. See the spec's Seed authority section._
7. **On transport loss and reconnect mid-match, how should the client resync to the stateful server's authoritative state for match_id — add a GetMatchState read RPC (recommended), or replay the action log? No read RPC returns a match's current state today (Init/Validate/Complete don't), so this needs a validator contract decision.** — _Resolved (Phase B). On transport loss the loop degrades to offline — the local deterministic engine remains the source of truth — and to go back online it re-inits the match (matching moveborne, which has no read RPC and recovers by re-registering current state). A dedicated GetMatchState(match_id, player_id) read RPC for true mid-match resume is an OPTIONAL later addition (War's server still holds the match under match_id), NOT required for v1; action-log replay is rejected as unnecessary. No longer a blocker._
8. **How do the transient beats (ROUND_RESULT / WAR_BANNER / WAR_REVEALING) hold their duration, given WarMatch emits engine events before state_changed and an award/decide returns the phase to dealt/war in the same apply — so a naive 'emit beat in _on_event, re-derive in _on_state' overwrites the transient instantly?** — _Buffer the last applied events and gate auto-advance from ONE place (_on_state). WarMatch emits events then state_changed; the Director records the events of the most-recent apply, and in _on_state — after re-deriving the resting beat — it FIRST holds the matching transient when those events warrant it: award → ROUND_RESULT for _dur('result'); tie → WAR_BANNER for _dur('war_banner', depth); war_decide → WAR_REVEALING for _dur('war_stake', depth). Only on that timer's expiry does it arm the resting beat's auto-action (start_round / resolve) or settle into WAR_PROMPT. A terminal phase short-circuits to GAME_OVER with no transient. The single armed timer carries the transient hold, so tap-to-skip (fire-now) and reconcile-cancel work uniformly. WAR_REVEALING is thus well-defined: it is the transient layered on the ack_war result (the war_decide event), after which a winner falls through to ROUND_RESULT and a re-tie loops back to WAR_BANNER/WAR_PROMPT at the higher depth._
9. **How is the online/validator workstream scoped and transported — split into the Validator Client feature, or phased inside this feature; and does the wire use the hand-rolled JSON dev-frame or generated godobuf bindings?** — _Phased inside this feature. Phase A = the offline-playable loop (Round Director, beats, tempo, war, replay), shippable on its own; Phase B = the validator wire, kept in this brief but clearly gated after Phase A. The wire uses generated godobuf bindings (tools/gen-protos.sh emits game/net/proto/*_pb.gd) carrying the real Hermes ClientMessage/ServerMessage with the inner ValidatorService request as protobuf bytes and game state/actions as canonical-JSON strings inside it; the hand-rolled JSON dev-frame is deprecated because it does not round-trip today (client encodes request_id/api_method/body, server reads method/requestId/args, replies under data). Watch godobuf field-name collisions (a field named 'message' fails to parse; generated has_method() collides with Object.has_method()) and rename the offending PROTO fields — field names are not on the wire, so renames are safe._
10. **Phase A edge case (presentation-only, surfaced in code review): when the match is WON on a war's deciding card, the engine produces [war_decide, award, game_over] in one apply, so the Round Director's terminal short-circuit routes straight to GAME_OVER and the WAR_REVEALING stake-reveal beat is skipped — the table jumps to the VICTORY/DEFEAT banner with no war animation. This honors the spec's 'a terminal award short-circuits straight to GAME_OVER' rule but skips the most dramatic reveal. Zero authority/hash impact; the detailed war animation is owned by the I-Declare-War Moment feature. Current behavior is locked by verify_play_loop._check_terminal_war_routes_game_over. Product call: keep the instant jump to game-over, or play the stake-reveal before the win banner on a game-winning war?** — _Keep the instant jump to GAME_OVER for the Core Play Loop. It honors the brief's 'a terminal award short-circuits straight to GAME_OVER' rule, is the simplest correct behavior, and has zero authority/hash impact (the action stream is unchanged). It is locked by verify_play_loop._check_terminal_war_routes_game_over. The dramatic stake-reveal-before-the-win-banner on a game-winning war is a presentation flourish that belongs to the I-Declare-War Moment feature, which owns the detailed WAR animation and already inserts pauses at the 'war' beats the Director exposes — so it can layer the deciding-card reveal there without touching this loop. Revisit during that feature if playtest shows the instant jump feels abrupt._
